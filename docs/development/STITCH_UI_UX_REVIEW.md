# Stitch UI/UX review against the shipped interface (19 Nov 2026)

**Status: review notes, not approved design decisions.** Nothing here changes code, a design file, a permission or an approved requirement. The new UI designs are on Stitch (project `15261718487894350517`, "Agricultural Operations Design System"; Figma is no longer the design source, owner direction 19 Nov 2026). Brand text in Stitch was **not** edited: the owner said the GoviAxis replacement can be done later.

## Coverage and method (read this first)

- The Stitch project has 74 items: 42 admin screens (A01 to A42), 18 field screens (F01 to F18), 11 loan-management screens (LM-01 to LM-11) and two specification documents.
- **Reviewed visually and by reading their HTML: three screens**, A01 Staff Sign-In, A06 Farmer Registry and F01 Field Entry and Device Sign-In. **The other 71 were not reviewed.** Findings below do not generalise to them without checking.
- Compared with the shipped admin sign-in page, screenshotted from a local development server on 19 Nov 2026 (so its error banner reflects no BFF being behind that server, not a defect). **The shipped farmer registry and field sign-in were not compared visually** (they need an authenticated, seeded stack); for them the comparison below rests on the code and the earlier UI work.
- This is one reviewer's reading of static screenshots: no usability test, no real device, no screen-reader or contrast measurement. Sinhala text in Stitch is unverified and needs native-speaker review.

## Verdict

The Stitch designs are **stronger than the shipped interface in information hierarchy, status vocabulary and field-first affordances**, and worth adopting selectively. They are **not shippable as drawn**: they carry claims the system does not make, disclose internal detail before sign-in, rely on approved-looking government identity that nobody approved, mix in out-of-scope features and are too dense on the registry. Adopt the structure; do not adopt the copy.

## What is weaker in the shipped interface (to improve, in a reviewed PR)

1. **Sign-in hierarchy.** Four same-weight fields, then three buttons: the two secondary actions ("Download device enrollment request", "Check session and refresh") compete with Sign in. Demote them to text links under the form.
2. **Brand presence.** A small dot and a name; the left panel is a large empty area. Add the GoviAxis mark and a bilingual line (the brand stays Latin script).
3. **First-load error.** An anonymous first visit can show a red error banner when the session probe fails with a server error. A normal "not signed in" response should be silent, and a service error should say what to do. Verify against a running stack before changing.
4. **Glass language on the sign-in surface.** The panel is flat solid green; the glass system used elsewhere is not visible here. Apply it with a solid fallback (UI rule 2).
5. **Status vocabulary** and a **visible scope banner** on the registry (adopt from A06).
6. **Pre-sign-in honesty.** The shipped page already avoids architecture and claims; keep it that way when adopting Stitch visuals.

## Decisions needed from the owner

1. Sign-in flow: keep the shipped single form (account, password, authenticator code, approved device) or move to Stitch's two steps (A01 then A02). This is an authentication change and needs security review.
2. Approved identity text for the sign-in and field screens (organisation and department naming), supplied by the client.
3. Whether any of the loan-management, harvest, boundary-survey or import/export screens enter scope.
4. Permission for a bulk export action and its limits.
5. Who validates the Sinhala text (native speaker), still unassigned.

## Suggested order of work (each a small reviewed PR, none started)

1. Correct the Stitch copy (S1 to S5, S7, S8) and re-export the three reviewed screens.
2. Sign-in visual refresh on the shipped single form (hierarchy, brand mark, demoted secondary actions, glass surface), keeping the current authentication flow.
3. Registry: status pills, provenance column, scope banner and permission codes for administrators, on the existing data; no new data fields without a requirement.
4. Field entry: compact status line and form-first layout.
5. Review the remaining 71 Stitch screens before relying on this document for them.

## Not claimed

No usability testing, no accessibility measurement, no real-device check and no client acceptance. The shipped registry and field pages were not compared visually. Gate G1 and production security acceptance are unaffected.
